PROJECT 001

Pedal-Powered
Alternator Generator

Energy Conversion & Mechanical Prototyping
STATUS: COMPLETE
YEAR: 2025
TOOLS: HAND TOOLS / ELECTRICAL / MULTIMETER
SITE: ST. MATTHEW'S SCHOOL

Funded, designed, and built a pedal-powered generator for classroom demonstrations — secured two grants totaling over $2,000 (CCNL's Youth Engagement Funding Initiative and the Wellness Coalition Avalon East) before touching any hardware.

Built around a genuine 12V/105A automotive alternator rather than a toy DC motor, belt-driven from a spin bike and mounted on a custom plywood-and-pallet base. Since the alternator outputs three-phase AC, a six-diode bridge rectifier converts it to usable DC, targeting ~1,500 RPM at the pedals.

A Raspberry Pi logs voltage, RPM, and power output during testing. Guarded moving parts, secure mounting, and insulated wiring throughout — built for regular use by children in a classroom.

FUNDING: $2,000+ ACROSS 2 GRANTS
DRIVE: BELT, SPIN BIKE TO ALTERNATOR
OUTPUT: RECTIFIED DC, ~1,500 RPM TARGET

Automated Door Closing Mechanism

Four-person team analyzing a four-bar linkage for automatic door closing. My role: concept design, dimensioning the linkage geometry (195 / 225 / 325 / 390 mm), and writing the results and conclusion. Kinematic analysis, Python script, and CAD model were teammates' work.

Project 004

Acoustic Tank Water Level Sensor

Built on direct supervisor request for the School of Ocean Technology's acoustic tank: detect a water-level drop and alert staff automatically. An HC-SR04 ultrasonic sensor, isolated inside a length of pipe, pairs with an ESP32 that emails an alert over Wi-Fi. Working prototype — calibration is next before permanent install.
